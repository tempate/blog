## The world

A _state-of-things_ is a combination of objects. I do not know how to define objects, so just think of them as the essence of the world.

An object has _internal_ and _external_ properties. Its external properties can change depending on the state-of-things of the object. The color of an object, for example, is an external property, since it depends on the state-of-things the object takes place in. 

An object's internal properties are its possibilities for combining with other objects. Each object contains all the possible states-of-things it can take place in, so it cannot exist in a vacuum. It can only exist in relation to other objects. Each object then contains the possibility of every state-of-things it can occur in. As an example, suppose that a human and a chair were objects. It is intrinsic to the definition of a chair that a human can sit on it, and it is intrinsic to the definition of a human that it can sit on a chair. The relation between a human and a chair is then inside the human and the chair. If we think of the human and the chair as vertices in the graph, then we could say that the vertices contain the edges. You cannot simply draw the vertex of a human. You also need to draw the chair and their relation.

The structure of a state-of-things is the relation between its objects. I like to think of a state-of-things as a graph in which vertices are objects and edges are structure. If we think of a state-of-things in which I am sitting on a chair, and we assume that the chair and I are objects, then the chair and I would be the vertices of the graph, and the edge would be our sitting relation.

_Reality_ is a function that maps states-of-things to the set {takes place, does not take place}. We call the states-of-things that take place in reality the _world_. Every state-of-things may or may not take place, so the world determines reality, because if we know all the states-of-things that take place, we also know all the states-of-things that do not. A state-of-things $X$ in which I have a cat is a valid state-of-things, but it is not part of the world, since I do not have a cat, so reality maps $X$ to "does not take place". A state-of-things $Y$ in which I have a dog is part of the world, since reality maps $Y$ to "takes place".

## Language

A _picture_ is a representation, also called a model. A _proposition_ is a picture of a state-of-things that may or may not take place, so propositions picture reality, not just the world. The proposition "the dog is sleeping on the couch" pictures a state-of-things in which the dog is sleeping on the couch. The dog may or may not be sleeping on the couch, so the proposition can be true or false, but it pictures the same state-of-things regardless.

_Language_ is the collection of all propositions. A _sign_ is the part of language we perceive, like a written or spoken word. A symbol is what different signs share when they mean the same thing. We call the symbol of an object its _name_.

In natural languages, many signs tend to share a symbol, and many symbols tend to share a sign. The written "screen", the spoken "screen", and the Spanish "pantalla" are three different signs for the same symbol. The sign "is" in the sentence "2+2 is 4" represents the symbol for equality, while in the sentence "there is a house with a garden" it represents the symbol for existence. In a perfect language, each symbol would have exactly one sign, and each sign exactly one symbol.

A _thought_ is a proposition that makes sense. The sentences "the good is identical to the beautiful" and "2+2 is 4" are not thoughts, because they do not picture a state-of-things. They are nonsense. The proposition "pigs can fly" is a thought, because it makes sense. 

_Natural science_ is the collection of true thoughts. The thought "pigs can fly" is not part of natural science, because it is not true, while the thought "birds can fly" is part of natural science, because it is true. 

_Philosophy_ is the clarification of thoughts. Philosophy is not a body of teachings, or a natural science, but an activity. The result of philosophy is not a collection of "philosophical propositions". It is just how a collection of propositions becomes clearer.


## The relation between language and the world

Suppose I photograph my room. I can recognize my room when I look at the photograph, so there must be something in common between the photograph and the room. They share a _spatial form_, since the objects in the photograph are arranged like the objects in the room.

A proposition pictures reality, so there must be something in common between the proposition and reality. The proposition "the dog is sleeping on the couch", however, is not arranged like a dog sleeping on a couch. What they have in common is more abstract. The dog and the couch stand in a certain relation in reality, and so do the words "dog" and "couch". We call whatever is in common between a picture and reality its _logical form_.

A proposition can never explicitly _say_ its logical form. It can only _show_ it. The logical form is not a state-of-things that may or may not take place, so a proposition cannot picture it. Language can then say how things stand in the world, but it cannot say what it shares with the world in order to say it.
